Taste

What I watch, read and keep going back to. This is my way of convincing you I have taste. By Aditi Sharma.

Watch list

  • Storyworthy: Explaining my 2023 hiatus. One of the best storytelling examples. I watch it every time I forget the rules of storytelling.
  • Tim Urban. Huge Tim Urban fan. Amazing storytelling, and I've been following his newsletter, Wait But Why, for a while.
  • Tom Scott. One of the most fun videos I've ever watched.
  • Tanmay Bhat on second-order effects. I've seen this idea explained a lot. I loved how Tanmay explained it.
  • Ananya's Instagram. A lot of my time goes into dissecting her storytelling. My favourite: this post.
  • Harvard Business Review. Back in 2023, this was my every-meal channel while I was learning to be a better leader and communicator.
  • Not Overthinking podcast. The reason my username is airwarmedd everywhere, and how I found a lot of the people I follow on X.
    • Episode. Signed up for the 10% pledge after watching this.
    • Episode
    • Episode. I've been going back to it since I started making video content.

Books

  • Big Magic. I don't usually reread books. I've reread this one three times already. The idea that stuck with me the last time: ideas are free-flowing. If you don't work on them when you have the time, they'll go to someone else.
  • Bird by Bird. My whole "let's do it and we can improve later" perspective comes from this book.
  • The 48 Laws of Power. I remember almost nothing because I read it in 2018, but I follow one rule: always say less than necessary. It's not the best read, honestly. There are still ways to look at it positively.
  • Maybe You Should Talk to Someone. I sent this to 8 of my friends. It opened up my vision and made me think about life more broadly.
  • Steve Jobs. My favourite biography. One day I want to write a biography, hopefully one like this. What I love about it: any learned skill is never wasted. I've done a bunch of random things, so a lot of it resonates.

Wait But Why

I'm a FAN of Tim Urban's writing.

  • How to Pick a Career. My absolute favourite. He walks you through how to think about your career: how to align your different personalities, wants, strengths and weaknesses, and eventually find what you want to do. It's a long, long exercise. I started it around the end of June and I'm still not done.
  • How to Pick Your Life Partner. Very fun. He dissects how people think, how we should think, what's realistic, and what we can do.
  • Mailbag #2. The most interesting one. He writes small essays as answers. My favourite question was "what really makes you angry." I spend a lot of time thinking about why before I do anything, so someone spending that much time on what makes them angry impresses me.

Newsletters

  • Shakti Shetty's Substack. I can't find the exact link, but he wrote a small story about how people expect others to act in the most moral way when they help them. He gave 50 rupees to someone in need, who bought a cheap quarter of alcohol with it. Others expected the man to do something good with it. But what? Fifty rupees won't make his life rich. It can make a few of his moments rich, in his own way. I think about that story a lot.
  • Matthew Dicks. He's been writing every day since 2007, and his newsletter lands in my inbox.
  • James Clear. I've been using his one question as my question of the week since 2023.
  • Marketing for Engineers. Written by a founder. I loved the "Integrated Developer Marketing" piece. It hurt a little because I come from a non-technical background. Still, I agree 100%. Early-stage tech startups need someone who can talk to customers, understand their pain points, and help engineers say what they're trying to say.
